Ireland in 10 days for under $1,800

A loop from Dublin to the Wild Atlantic Way, with a real cost table, a daily route that doesn't require a rally license, and the three splurges actually worth paying for. The ground costs come in under $1,800 per person; flights are the variable.

Ireland is one of those destinations where the brochure version and the budget version are almost the same trip. The cliffs don't charge admission. The trad sessions don't have a VIP section. The best drive in the country — the Slea Head loop — is free, provided you're comfortable sharing a single-track road with a sheep.

What costs money is the boring stuff: the car, the beds, the fuel, and the food. Get those right and a 10-day loop can come in under $1,800 per person, excluding transatlantic airfare. Add a shoulder-season flight from the East Coast and you're looking at roughly $2,200–$2,400 all-in — still less than a week at a mid-range resort.

The $1,800 math

This plan assumes two people sharing a double room and a compact car, traveling in May, September, or October. It excludes flights, which vary too much to pretend otherwise, and it includes one proper dinner, one boat trip, and enough pints to be sociable.

That's more comfortable than the headline suggests. The $1,800 ceiling is per person, and the baseline above leaves room for one significant splurge (Skellig Michael, a sea-view room, or a very good dinner) without breaking it.

The 10-day route

The loop runs Dublin → Galway → Doolin → Dingle → Kenmare → Kinsale → Dublin. It's about 1,100 km of driving, with the longest day around three hours. You can do it in either direction; this way keeps the best scenery for the middle of the trip.

Day 1 — Dublin, gently

Land, collect the car if you're picking it up at the airport, or wait until morning and take the bus into town. Walk Trinity College, the National Museum of Ireland (free), and St Stephen's Green. Dinner somewhere unpretentious — a pub with a stew and a pint is the correct arrival meal. Sleep in Dublin.

Day 2 — Dublin to Galway

Drive west (about 2.5 hours), stopping at Clonmacnoise, the sixth-century monastic site on the Shannon. It's a small detour and a good antidote to city traffic. Arrive in Galway for the evening: the Latin Quarter, the Claddagh, and a session in a pub that isn't advertising one. Two nights in Galway.

Day 3 — Connemara loop

A day trip west: Maam Cross, Kylemore Abbey (optional, about €17), the Sky Road above Clifden, and Roundstone for a late lunch. If the weather is doing its dramatic thing, this is the day to point the car at it. Back to Galway for dinner.

Day 4 — Galway to Doolin

Drive south through the Burren — a lunar landscape of limestone pavement that looks nothing like the postcard version of Ireland. Stop at the Cliffs of Moher in the late afternoon, when the tour buses have thinned and the light is better. Stay in Doolin, where the trad music is the whole point. One night.

Day 5 — Doolin to Dingle

Take the Shannon ferry (about €20 for a car) or drive via Limerick if the ferry isn't running. Either way, you'll reach Dingle in two to three hours. Walk the harbor, find a pub with a fire, and accept that you will hear “The Wild Rover” at least once. Two nights in Dingle.

Day 6 — Slea Head Drive

The 30-mile loop around Slea Head is the best free thing in Ireland: stone beehive huts, sheer cliffs, beaches that look Caribbean until you touch the water, and views toward the Blasket Islands. Go early or late to avoid the coach traffic. If the weather is clear, add a boat trip to the Blaskets (about €35).

Day 7 — Dingle to Kenmare

Cross the Conor Pass if you're confident on narrow roads — it's spectacular and not for everyone. Otherwise take the longer route via Tralee. Stop at Inch Beach, then cut through Killarney National Park for Torc Waterfall and the lakes. Sleep in Kenmare, a small town with better food than it has any right to have.

Day 8 — Kenmare to Kinsale

Drive the Ring of Kerry if you want the full loop (about 4 hours with stops), or skip it for a gentler run to Kinsale via Bantry and the coast. Kinsale is colorful, walkable, and has the best seafood of the trip. Visit Charles Fort if you have daylight. One night.

Day 9 — Kinsale to Dublin

Drive back east (about 3 hours), stopping at the Rock of Cashel — the limestone fortress where the kings of Munster did their kinging. Arrive in Dublin for a last night, ideally somewhere central enough to walk to dinner.

Day 10 — Dublin and out

If your flight is later, take the DART to Howth for a cliff walk and a seafood lunch, or do the Guinness Storehouse if you haven't done a distillery yet. Return the car with time to spare; Irish airport rental returns are not the place to discover a missing fuel receipt.

What it actually costs

The cost table above is the plan. Here's where people blow it:

  • Dublin hotels. A “cheap” Dublin room in peak season is $200+. Book a guesthouse outside the center or stay the first night near the airport and drive west in the morning.
  • Car insurance. The base rate is never the real rate. Either take the full excess waiver or buy a separate excess policy in advance — the latter is usually cheaper, but read the exclusions.
  • Eating in tourist towns. Dingle and Kinsale are lovely and priced accordingly. One nice meal, otherwise eat where the locals eat, or self-cater breakfast.
  • Attraction creep. €12 here, €17 there, and suddenly you've spent $200 on visitor centers. Pick two paid attractions and let the landscape do the rest.

Three splurges worth it

  1. Skellig Michael landing (about €125). A UNESCO-listed monastic island 12 km off the Kerry coast. The boat ride is rough, the steps are steep, and the weather cancels more trips than it allows. Book months ahead and treat it as weather-dependent. If it runs, it's the memory you'll keep.
  2. One sea-view room in Dingle (about +$40). Not the whole trip — one night. Wake up to the harbor, walk to breakfast, and remember why you came.
  3. A proper dinner in Kinsale (about $60 per person). The town has an unusual density of good kitchens. Pick one, order the fish, and skip the souvenir shops the next morning.

What to skip

  • The Blarney Castle kiss. The queue is long, the stone is not magic, and the gardens are the actual reason to go.
  • Coached Ring of Kerry tours. You'll spend more time in a bus than looking at the view. Drive it yourself or skip it.
  • The Guinness Storehouse if you've already done a distillery. It's well done and expensive; one branded experience per trip is plenty.
  • Overplanning. Ireland's weather rewrites itineraries hourly. Leave one day loose.

Practical notes

  • Drive on the left. Get the smallest car you can live with. The roads are narrow, the hedges are close, and the sheep have right of way.
  • Book the car early. Shoulder season is fine, but automatics and small cars sell out first. Check the excess and fuel policy before you pay.
  • Bring layers, not an umbrella. Wind makes umbrellas a comedy prop. A waterproof shell and warm mid-layer will do more.
  • Check seasonal opening hours. Some smaller attractions and restaurants close outside summer. A quick call saves a wasted detour.
  • Use the ferry. The Shannon crossing saves time and is a pleasant 20 minutes. Check the last sailing before you commit.
  • Budget in euros. Cards are widely accepted, but small pubs and parking meters still like cash.

Ireland rewards the traveler who slows down. The itinerary above is a frame, not a checklist. If the sun comes out on the Dingle Peninsula, stay another hour. The cliffs will still be there.
